Introductions follow six paragraphs, one job each: (1) the general economic question, the gap in the literature, and this paper's research question; (2) what we do (data, design, model); (3) main findings, in several folds (First, Second, Third); (4) contribution relative to the literature; (5) implication (policy or economic meaning); (6) roadmap.

**Why:** On 2026-09-23 (Carbon Pricing Policy) an eleven-paragraph AER-formula introduction with a separate hook, design, margins, timing, model, fit and three literature paragraphs was rejected as "too long, does not follow the neatness of academic writing." The author wants each paragraph to carry one function and the whole to fit in about a page and a half.

**How to apply:** Draft introductions to these six slots before anything else; fold hooks, design details and fit statistics into the slot they serve rather than giving them paragraphs. Contribution goes in one paragraph, not one per literature. Keep the aer-introduction skill's surface rules (no math, no forward references outside the roadmap) but its paragraph count yields to this structure.
