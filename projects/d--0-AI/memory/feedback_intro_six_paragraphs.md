Before rewriting an introduction, read the introductions of closely related published papers and take the structure from them. Do not hold the introduction to six paragraphs. Order of priorities: (1) strong motivation and a clear presentation of the results, (2) then framing for the target journal.

**Why:** On 2026-09-23 (Carbon Pricing Policy) the author rejected an eleven-paragraph AER-formula introduction as too long and not neat, and I recorded a six-paragraph rule (question+gap+RQ, what we do, findings, contribution, implication, roadmap). On 2026-11-16, same project, the author corrected this: "we need to check relevant studies and paper on how they write the introduction, other than stick to the 6 paragraph rule. most of top papers have more than 6 paragraphs", and "first make sure we motivate strongly and present our results clearly, then the frame to JDE style". The compression to six paragraphs had also cut literature the coauthor wrote, which the author wanted kept (moved to the contribution discussion). My interpretation: the 2026-09-23 objection was to sprawl and to paragraphs without one clear job, not to the count.

**How to apply:** Map the introductions of the closest published papers (paragraph functions, where results and literature sit, length) and propose a structure from that evidence before drafting. Each paragraph still carries one function. Keep relevant literature the coauthors wrote, relocating it if needed instead of deleting it. Do journal-specific framing only after motivation and results read well.
