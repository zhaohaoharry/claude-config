Manuscript prose is written for a human reader who holds only the paper, not for an agent that already has the whole project in context. Every sentence must be a full paper sentence that says what the object is, what is done with it, and why, in the words the reader has already been given.

**Why:** The user read a Section 5 paragraph ("The carbon price itself is measured, not estimated. ... with the potentials, the access costs and nu_k in hand the model predicts ... This prediction is a test of the model. Solving ... and again under the measured charge, and pooling ..., gives model counterparts of the four facts, and their comparison with the estimates is the first result of the estimation.") and said it reads like AI notes for AI: clipped verdict sentences, compressed lists of objects, references to things "in hand", and pronouns or labels ("the four facts", "the response") that assume the reader remembers everything. This is a global rule for all manuscripts.

**How to apply:**
- Write complete sentences with a subject, a verb and the object named in words; do not compress a step into a label ("the charge-only prediction") or a verdict ("This prediction is a test of the model.").
- Do not assume the reader recalls earlier objects; re-state what a thing is when it reappears ("the response of import links to adoption that Fact 4 documents", not "the four facts").
- No "X, not Y" contrast sentences, no "with A, B and C in hand", no "gives model counterparts of", no chains of nominalizations; say who does what to what.
- Avoid pointer language as a substitute for explanation (equation numbers or section references carrying the meaning of a sentence); the sentence must make sense with the pointer removed.
