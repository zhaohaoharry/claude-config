When a paper is retargeted to a different journal, the accurate description of what the study does stays constant. Only the emphasis shifts: which existing element is made salient first. Never restate the paper's subject as the journal's pet topic.

Concrete case (2026-09-20, Governor and Border Emissions, IER rejection then JUE). The paper examines what happens when a principal appoints a monitored agent as supervisor, tested on pollution at Chinese provincial borders. For IER the attribution problem was made salient. For JUE the jurisdictional boundary was. I wrote a JUE cover letter opening "Our study examines how polluting activity is distributed across a jurisdictional border, and what changes that distribution." The author rejected it: "the boundary is just to make more salient, not to make it all ... we still tell what we do accurately, but with emphasis from different angles."

**Why:** an opening that redefines the paper as being about the journal's topic is inaccurate, and referees who read the manuscript will see the mismatch between the letter and the paper. It also throws away the contribution that makes the paper more than a topic paper.

**How to apply:** keep the sentence that states what the study does intact, then foreground the target journal's angle as the setting or the testing ground in the same sentence or the next one. Accepted fix: "Our study examines what happens when a central government appoints one of the local officials it monitors to a supervisory role, and it tests the consequences at provincial borders, where pollution crosses from one jurisdiction into the next."
