Do not write super long paragraphs in manuscripts. Split a block into paragraphs by topic: one paragraph per idea (for example, the list of moments; then the identification of each parameter; then the solution method). A paragraph that runs half a page or more is acceptable only when it cannot be split without breaking one argument.

**Why:** The user pointed at the Section 5.3 paragraph "The moments that the estimation matches are the counterparts..." (moments list, identification of four parameters, and the solver, all in one block of about 20 sentences) and made it a global rule for all writing.

**How to apply:**
- After drafting, scan each paragraph; if it covers more than one topic or exceeds roughly 8-10 sentences, split it at the topic change and give each part its own topic sentence.
- Lists of items (moments, parameters, steps) get their own paragraph; the explanation of what identifies each parameter gets another; a solution or computational note gets a third.
