The user rejects "loose" sentences in manuscripts: conversational or clipped phrasing ("their value is a transfer", "most of them pricing X first, and in their prices", "Dealing with a foreign origin is not free", "It does so at a price", "in hand", "which one wins", "the contest"), mechanical agent phrasing ("the equilibrium lets it move", "the budget pins the level"), and words that are rare in top journals ("dearer"). Passive voice is fine and often preferred ("the wage is determined by", "prices are converted to").

**Why:** Such sentences read as notes or as an AI draft, not as a paper; economics prose states things plainly and completely, in the vocabulary of the field.

**How to apply:** Write every manuscript sentence as it would appear in a published paper: complete, literal, standard vocabulary, one clear subject and verb. Prefer "X is determined by Y" to "Y pins X"; "which effect dominates depends on" to "which one wins"; "more expensive" to "dearer". Data sections describe data and policy only, never the model.
