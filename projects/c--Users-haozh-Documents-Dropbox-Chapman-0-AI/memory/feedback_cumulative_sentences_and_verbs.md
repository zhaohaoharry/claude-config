Two prose rules the user stated directly while revising the IER paper (2026-08-22), both of which override or sharpen the written guides.

**1. No sentence may open by restating the previous sentence.** If a short sentence's subject is just the prior sentence's content ("This bilateral distortion...", "Imperfect attribution encourages...", "The same role allows..."), fold it into that sentence as a subordinate clause, an appositive, or a "thereby / since / so" continuation. The user's words: "I don't like sentences start by repeating the last sentence when they can combine to a complex sentence with more clear meaning." Short standalone sentences carrying meaning already connected to the previous one read as seams, not rhythm.

**Why:** it produces the cumulative, subordinating prose of top econ journals instead of a chain of declaratives with anaphoric subjects.

**How to apply:** this overrides the "sentences over 30 words are almost always rewritable" guidance in `AI_Writing_Guide_Academic.md` section 2.0. Cumulative sentences of 40 to 50 words are correct here when each clause adds new information. The guides' surface rules (no em-dash, colon, semicolon) still bind, so carry the subordination with commas and conjunctions.

**Quantitative target, measured on the user's own accepted paragraphs (2026-08-22).** Body paragraphs run four to five sentences averaging about 40 words, built as one main clause plus a chain of subordinate clauses (`where …`, `since …`, `so that …`, `which …`, `while …`, `thereby -ing`). A rejected draft of the same content ran seven sentences averaging 21 words including a 7-word standalone, and the user called it "awful," because splitting a qualification, a corroboration, or a consequence into its own sentence turns an argument into a list of findings.

**Checklist before saving any results or mechanism paragraph:** no sentence under roughly 25 words; no paragraph over five sentences; and no fact given its own sentence when it qualifies, corroborates, or follows from the previous one. Fold those into subordinate clauses. The one licensed exception is a deliberate short verdict after a long setup, capped at once per paper.

**2. Verb energy is how the user judges whether prose sounds human.** The user rewrote a paragraph and said the AI version "reads very bad" by comparison. The actual difference was verbs: the user wrote *reap, export, lock, shelter*; the draft had *capture, bear, allow, give*. Signed, physical, field-specific verbs read as a specific economist. Neutral ones read as a machine. Field idiom matters at the same level, for example "individual efforts" (Holmstrom's team-production term) rather than "individual actions" when invoking an attribution or moral-hazard-in-teams friction.

**How to apply:** before saving econ prose, sweep the main verbs. If they are all neutral (is, gives, allows, has, provides, captures), the paragraph will read as generated no matter how clean it is. See [[econ_craft_system]] for the guides this sharpens.

Note that the user's own drafts often break the punctuation rules (em-dashes, colons) and reintroduce words they earlier retired. Adopt their substance and verve, repair the surface silently, and say what was repaired.
