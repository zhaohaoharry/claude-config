Every statement about a real paper, official document or policy (what it does, finds, or how it differs from ours) must come from the source itself: the PDF is in `literature/` (or the document is saved in the project), and it has been read through in full in the current work before the argument is written or proposed. Never from background knowledge, never from an abstract alone. If the source is missing, first search for and download it yourself (Google Scholar, publisher, NBER/SSRN, author sites, official .gov.cn pages; legal copies only) before asking the user. Only what cannot be obtained goes to the user, listed with full authors, year, exact title and journal. Prefer the published version; a WP-only copy of a published paper also goes on the list.

**Why:** 2026-11-26 the user caught me characterizing Bronnenberg et al. (2012) and Coşar et al. / Evans from memory in the intro's literature argument; claims about the literature establish the contribution, so they must be exact. The user called this "the settled rule we must follow for every study."

**How to apply:** before writing or editing any sentence that cites or describes a source (intro, literature paragraph, institutional facts such as insurance-pool rules), check the PDF exists, read it fully, then write. In chat, label anything not yet read as unread. Applies across all projects. Related: [[feedback_no_defensive_writing]].
