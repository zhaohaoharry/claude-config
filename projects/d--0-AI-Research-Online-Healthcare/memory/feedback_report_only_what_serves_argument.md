Main-text prose should state each result at the level that serves the paper's key argument, and stop there. Never misreport, but do not report everything: a correct side result that needs the reader to follow an underlying calculation (e.g., tier-specific variances making the mean choice weight of level-2 and level-3A hospitals equal) is left out of the main text, its table row and the abstract/conclusion. The user calls this the general rule of thesis writing.

**Why:** On 2026-10-22, the "expected attractiveness of a random level-2 hospital equals level 3A" sentence confused the user, and its explanation, though right, did not serve the claim that level-3A hospitals are more attractive.

**How to apply:** Before adding a nuance, caveat or decomposition to the main text, ask whether the key finding needs it. If it only adds machinery, omit it, or move it to the appendix if a referee needs it. Applies alongside the user's writing guides.
