On 2026-11-16 the user rewrote the introduction of `latex/manuscript.tex` themselves and said it "has the flow and economic writing sense I want ... make sure you write like this". Features I (Claude) read off that rewrite:
- P1 opens on a general economic principle: expertise in knowledge-intensive services concentrates in big centers, but face-to-face delivery bounds access. It then states the policy trade-off (move consumers or move experts), names it in trade theory (proximity-concentration) and ends on the empirical question (friction or preference). The setting (China, Beijing/Shanghai) enters only in P2 as the sharpest case.
- General-interest nouns and confident, signed verbs: exploit, unlock, fragment, strip away, harness, decouple.
- If/if/however-if chains to lay out policy stakes. "We exploit this setting in three steps. First... Second... Third..." Contributions are "First, we contribute to X. While existing studies..., we...". The closest-paper paragraph is "We advance beyond their analysis in three dimensions".
- Long cumulative sentences with participial modifiers.

**Why:** this is the user's own accepted text. It is grander and more general than the 2026-10-25 plain-register note, see [[feedback-plain-register]]. For framing passages it takes precedence over that note.

**How to apply:** match this register in intro, abstract and conclusion framing. Keep factual discipline anyway: macros for numbers, no claims beyond the results, and no superlatives that a referee can contest.

The rewrite uses em-dashes and semicolons, which conflicts with the standing no-dash rule. Whether that rule is relaxed was not settled on 2026-11-16, so ask before adding new ones.
