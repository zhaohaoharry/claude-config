Write cumulative sentences (one main clause plus a chain of subordinate clauses) and never open a sentence, or a paragraph, with a short declarative that only restates the previous sentence or paragraph.

**Why:** On 2026-08-27, reviewing the Water and Crop introduction, the author rejected "That remedy has become policy." as a paragraph opener: "I especially hate short sentence like this ... which contain no transition ... or just simple repeat previous sentence. The writing should be: no redundant and repeat, precise, neat, transition well." Earlier the same day he rejected the label-then-explanation pattern ("First, leakage is rarely measured. The resource that leaks ...") as "sparse sentences." He also rejects over-long drafts: "you always write more than I want ... I want things to be neat. I still need long sentence with clause to deliver a complete meaning with transition inside the sentence."

**How to apply:**
- A bridge between paragraphs goes *inside* the first sentence as a subordinate clause: "As that remedy has spread into policy, with tradable permits and water markets now allocating ..., the leakage it induces ... raises the question ...". Not "That remedy has become policy. Tradable permits ...".
- "This paper provides a quantitative answer." becomes "This paper provides a quantitative answer by turning to X, a setting that clears all three hurdles, since ...".
- Enumerated reasons carry the label and the reason in one sentence ("The primary reason is that ..., so ..."; "Moreover, ..."; "Lastly, ...").
- Cut before adding: each rewrite should be shorter than the version it replaces unless new content is required.
- The one licensed short sentence is a verdict after a long setup, at most once per paper.
