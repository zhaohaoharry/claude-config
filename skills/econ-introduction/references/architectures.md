# Introduction architectures by paper type

Use these as default belief sequences, not fixed templates. Combine or reorder paragraphs when the paper's logic requires it, but preserve the progression from consequential question to credible answer to changed knowledge.

## Reduced-form empirical paper

1. Establish a broad economic problem, isolate what remains unknown, and pose the research question.
2. Introduce the setting and source of identifying variation. Explain why this variation answers the question and address the dominant threat in one direct argument.
3. State the main estimates with economically interpretable magnitudes and explain what they imply.
4. Use mechanism evidence to distinguish competing explanations rather than to add another outcome.
5. Use heterogeneity to establish incidence, scope, or a theoretically meaningful boundary condition.
6. Include extensions only when they alter the main interpretation or external relevance.
7. Position the result against the closest literature and state what readers now know that they did not know before.
8. End with a short roadmap if useful.

For an instrumental-variables paper, name the instrument and give the exclusion logic in words on the first page. Save variable construction, equations, first-stage tables, and full diagnostics for the body.

## Structural or quantitative paper

1. State the economic decision or policy counterfactual that cannot be answered from reduced-form evidence alone.
2. Explain the economic mechanism that makes the counterfactual difficult, including the key equilibrium or selection force.
3. Describe the model in economic language. Identify which data moments or quasi-experimental variation discipline the parameters that matter for the counterfactual.
4. Report model fit or validation only to the extent needed to make the counterfactual credible.
5. State the main quantitative result with a benchmark and explain which mechanism drives it.
6. Use decompositions and counterfactuals to escalate one argument, not to display the full model menu.
7. Explain how the paper changes a policy calculation, a measured sufficient statistic, or a prevailing interpretation.

## Theory paper

1. Begin with a canonical economic problem, a contradiction between existing results, or a phenomenon that current models cannot jointly explain.
2. Pose the precise theoretical question and explain why the missing margin matters.
3. Introduce the minimal model innovation in words and explain the mechanism before notation.
4. State the main result or proposition with its intuition, scope, and economically meaningful comparative static.
5. Show how later results deepen the same mechanism through generalization, welfare, implementation, or application.
6. Relate the model to the closest theories by identifying which conclusion changes and why, not merely which assumption differs.
7. Use an empirical example only to make the mechanism concrete unless the paper formally tests it.

## Macro or quantitative macro paper

1. Establish an aggregate fact, policy problem, or unresolved quantitative discrepancy.
2. State the economic question in terms of a magnitude, propagation mechanism, incidence, or counterfactual.
3. Explain the mechanism and the source of discipline, separating what the data identify from what the model extrapolates.
4. Present the main aggregate result alongside a familiar benchmark or canonical calculation.
5. Use decompositions to show which mechanism accounts for the result and which plausible alternatives do not.
6. Explain the policy or welfare consequence without implying more external validity than the model and evidence support.
7. Position the paper by showing which established macro conclusion, calibration target, or policy calculation changes.

## Mixed theory and empirical paper

Do not write a theory introduction followed by an empirical introduction. Use one question. Introduce the empirical puzzle, explain why competing mechanisms imply different observable patterns, and show how the model turns those patterns into discriminating tests. Report the empirical sign or contrast that selects among mechanisms, then explain what the combined evidence establishes that neither component could establish alone.
